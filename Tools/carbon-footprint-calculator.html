How the estimate is calculated

Each activity is multiplied by an emission factor — the amount of carbon dioxide (or CO₂ equivalent) released per unit. The factors below come from the U.S. EPA. All results are approximate.

Notes on accuracy

  • Electricity varies the most. Depending on your local grid, the factor can range from roughly 0.2 to more than 0.6 kg per kWh. Choose the closest option above, or enter your utility's own figure.
  • Flight factors are averages across aircraft and seating. The EPA figures used here don't clearly include the extra warming effect of contrails at altitude, so real climate impact may be higher.
  • Gasoline and diesel factors count CO₂ only — the small methane and nitrous oxide emissions from tailpipes are left out.

Frequently asked questions

Why does the result leave out food and shopping?

Those emissions can't be measured from a bill or an odometer, and estimates for them vary widely by source. Keeping to energy and travel means every number here traces to a published emission factor.

Is CO₂ the same as CO₂e?

CO₂e ("carbon dioxide equivalent") bundles other greenhouse gases like methane into a single number using their warming strength. Fuel factors here are mostly CO₂; the electricity and flight factors are CO₂e.

How can I find my grid's emission factor?

The EPA's eGRID publishes emission rates by region. Some utilities also list their own rate on their website or bill.

What's the fastest way to lower my number?

Look at your biggest category first. For many households that's driving or home heating; for frequent flyers it's often flights. The results below show your largest source and what a 10% cut would save.
