How school-entry age works

In most U.S. districts a child starts kindergarten in the fall they turn 5 — meaning they must be 5 on or before a set cutoff date, commonly September 1. A child who turns 5 the day after the cutoff generally waits a full year, which is why kids in the same grade can be almost a year apart in age.

This calculator counts a child's age on the cutoff date and maps it to the usual grade: age 5 at the cutoff is kindergarten, age 6 is first grade, and so on up to age 17 in twelfth grade. It also shows how many days the fifth birthday falls before or after the cutoff, so you can see whether a child would be among the oldest or youngest in the class.

Frequently asked questions

What is a school age cutoff date?

It's the date by which a child must reach a minimum age to enroll in a given grade. Many districts use September 1, but some use other dates such as August 31, September 30, or December 1.

What if my child's birthday is right after the cutoff?

They'll typically start a year later and be among the oldest in their class. Some districts allow early-entry testing or waivers — ask your school.

Can a child be in a different grade than this predicts?

Yes. Families sometimes delay kindergarten ("redshirting"), schools sometimes accelerate or hold students back, and districts can have their own rules.

Does this work outside the U.S.?

The age-to-grade pattern here is the common U.S. one. Other countries use different school-year calendars, cutoff dates, and grade names.
