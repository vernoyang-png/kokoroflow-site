Driving guide

How to compare MPG and L/100km correctly

The units do not only use different scales—they reward opposite directions.

Reviewed: October 2026 · 5 minute read

Three common fuel-economy units

US MPG measures miles traveled per US gallon. Imperial MPG uses the larger Imperial gallon, so the same car receives a higher Imperial MPG number. L/100km measures liters consumed over 100 kilometers. Higher MPG means better economy, while lower L/100km means better economy.

Useful conversion formulas

US MPG to L/100km: 235.214583 ÷ US MPG.
Imperial MPG to L/100km: 282.480936 ÷ Imperial MPG.

Because the relationship is reciprocal, a change of five MPG does not always produce the same change in L/100km. Compare the converted fuel consumption rather than comparing the number difference alone.

Worked example

A vehicle rated at 30 US MPG converts to about 7.84 L/100km and 36.0 Imperial MPG. Another vehicle rated at 6.5 L/100km converts to about 36.2 US MPG. The second vehicle uses less fuel under the stated test figures.

Estimate fuel cost separately

Fuel economy is not fuel cost. To estimate a trip, convert the driving distance into the matching unit, calculate the fuel quantity, then multiply by the current local price. Include a realistic buffer for traffic, weather, hills, luggage, and driving style.

Ratings versus real use

Official tests make cars easier to compare, but real consumption varies. Vehicle condition, tire pressure, speed, temperature, heating or air-conditioning, route, and load can all matter. Use the same source and test standard when comparing two models.

In depth

Going further: turning fuel economy into dollars

Fuel economy numbers matter because of what they cost. Converting them to gallons used and dollars per year makes comparisons clearer, and it explains why some MPG improvements save far more than others.

Cost per mile and per year

Fuel cost per mile = fuel price ÷ MPG. Annual fuel cost = miles per year ÷ MPG × fuel price. At an example price of $3.50 a gallon and 12,000 miles a year:

VehicleGallons per yearAnnual fuel costCost per mile
25 MPG480$1,680$0.14
35 MPGabout 343$1,200$0.10

The difference is $480 a year at that price. Use your own local fuel price and mileage, because both change the answer.

The MPG illusion

Because MPG is miles per gallon rather than gallons per mile, equal MPG gains don't save equal fuel. Over 10,000 miles:

  • 10 to 20 MPG cuts fuel use from 1,000 to 500 gallons, saving 500 gallons.
  • 30 to 50 MPG cuts fuel use from about 333 to 200 gallons, saving about 133 gallons.

Replacing the least efficient vehicle in a household often saves more fuel than upgrading the most efficient one. That is why L/100 km and gallons per 100 miles, which measure fuel used, are easier to compare.

Measuring your real MPG

  1. Fill the tank and reset the trip meter.
  2. Drive normally until the next fill-up.
  3. Divide miles driven by the gallons needed to refill. Average several tanks for a more reliable number.

Real-world results usually differ from the official label. Cold weather, short trips, high speeds and heavy loads tend to lower them.
