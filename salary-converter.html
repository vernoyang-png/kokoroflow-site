Estimate gross pay

What this estimate includes

The annual result is hourly rate × weekly hours × paid weeks. Monthly pay is the annual figure divided by 12. It is a gross estimate before income tax, social contributions, pension deductions, benefits, unpaid leave, bonuses, or overtime premiums.

Compare jobs carefully

An employee and a contractor with the same headline hourly rate may not receive the same overall value. Compare paid leave, insurance, retirement contributions, equipment, non-billable time, and employment taxes.

In depth

Converting hourly pay to salary without the usual mistakes

The basic conversion is simple multiplication. The mistakes come from the assumptions hidden inside it. This section shows the standard rule of thumb, where it goes wrong, and how to compare an hourly job with a salaried one fairly.

The 2,080-hour rule of thumb

A full-time year is often treated as 40 hours × 52 weeks = 2,080 hours. That gives two quick shortcuts: multiply an hourly rate by about 2,000 to estimate annual pay, or divide a salary by 2,080 to get an hourly equivalent.

The middle column assumes two unpaid weeks. If your hourly job has no paid holidays or vacation, that column is closer to reality than the 52-week figure.

Mistake 1: treating a month as four weeks

A year has 52 weeks but only 48 "four-week months". Multiplying weekly pay by four undercounts income by about 8%. Divide the annual total by 12 for a true monthly average, as the converter does.

Mistake 2: ignoring overtime rules

Under the federal Fair Labor Standards Act, non-exempt employees must be paid at least one and one-half times their regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek. A $25-an-hour worker who regularly works 45 hours earns 40 × $25 + 5 × $37.50 = $1,187.50 a week, or $61,750 over 52 weeks. A salaried offer of $55,000 with the same hours would actually pay less. Exempt salaried employees generally do not receive overtime.

Mistake 3: comparing pay without benefits

  • Paid time off: ten paid holidays plus two weeks of vacation is roughly 120 paid hours that an hourly contractor would not receive.
  • Retirement match: a 4% 401(k) match on $60,000 is worth $2,400 a year.
  • Health insurance: the employer share of premiums is often worth thousands of dollars a year.
  • Self-employment tax: a 1099 contractor pays both halves of Social Security and Medicare (15.3% on most earnings), so the same hourly rate is worth less than it is for a W-2 employee.
