Why real-world economy differs from the window sticker

EPA fuel economy ratings are estimated under standardized lab conditions. Real-world mileage is affected by driving style, terrain, weather, tire pressure, and how much weight the vehicle is carrying, so calculating your actual economy from a real trip is often more useful for budgeting than the official rating alone.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate MPG from a fill-up?
Reset your trip odometer at a fill-up, drive normally until your next fill-up, then divide the miles driven by the gallons it took to fill the tank back up. This gives a more accurate real-world number than a single tank estimate, since it accounts for a full driving cycle.

What's the difference between MPG and L/100km?
MPG (miles per gallon) is a "bigger number is better" measure common in the US, while L/100km (liters per 100 kilometers) is a "smaller number is better" measure common internationally — they measure the same underlying efficiency, just framed in opposite directions.

Why compare two vehicles by annual cost instead of just MPG?
A jump from 45 to 60 MPG saves less fuel over a year than a jump from 15 to 20 MPG, even though both are "5 MPG better" — because fuel savings scale with gallons used, not the MPG number itself. Comparing projected annual cost avoids this common misreading.
