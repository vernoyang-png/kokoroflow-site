Work and money guide

Compare hourly pay and annual salary without hiding the assumptions

The right conversion depends on actual weekly hours and the number of weeks that are paid.

Reviewed: October 2026 · 5 minute read

The basic gross-pay formula

Annual gross pay = hourly rate × hours per week × paid weeks per year.

At 25 per hour, 40 hours per week, and 52 paid weeks, the simple annual estimate is 52,000. If four weeks are unpaid, using 48 paid weeks produces 48,000.

Monthly pay is not four weekly payments

A calendar year contains more than 48 weeks. For a consistent monthly average, divide annual pay by 12. For weekly budgeting, use the weekly figure; for a monthly comparison, use the annual figure divided by 12.

Gross is not take-home pay

The converter does not calculate income tax, national insurance or social contributions, pension deductions, health insurance, student loans, or other payroll items. These rules vary by country and personal circumstances. Use an official calculator or payslip for a net-pay estimate.

Compare total compensation

When comparing an hourly role with a salaried role, include paid leave, sick leave, retirement contributions, insurance, bonuses, overtime treatment, schedule stability, travel time, and equipment. A higher cash rate may not be a better total package.

Contractor rates require another calculation

A self-employed worker may pay business costs and spend substantial time on administration or marketing. Do not treat an employee hourly rate as an equivalent freelance billing rate. Use the Freelance Rate Calculator for that comparison.

In depth

Going further: overtime, benefits and contract pay

The formula above converts pay accurately, but a fair job comparison in the U.S. also has to account for overtime law, paid time off, benefits and payroll taxes. These are the adjustments that most often flip which offer is better.

Overtime for non-exempt workers

Under the federal Fair Labor Standards Act, non-exempt employees must receive at least 1.5 times their regular rate for hours worked over 40 in a workweek. At $24 an hour and 46 hours a week, weekly pay is 40 × $24 + 6 × $36 = $1,176, which comes to $61,152 over 52 weeks. A salaried "exempt" role at $56,000 with the same hours pays less, even though the headline number looks similar. Some states set stricter rules, such as daily overtime.

Put a dollar value on paid time off

Paid holidays and vacation are pay for hours you don't work. Ten holidays plus ten vacation days is 160 paid hours. At $30 an hour, that is $4,800 a year that an hourly job without PTO wouldn't pay you. If you are comparing a contract with no PTO against a salaried role, count only the weeks you would really work in the contract.

Benefits are part of the offer

  • Retirement match. A 50% match on the first 6% of a $60,000 salary is $1,800 a year.
  • Health insurance. Compare your share of the premium and the deductible, not just whether insurance is offered.
  • Other benefits. Commuter benefits, tuition support and equipment allowances also have real cash value.

W-2 employee vs. 1099 contractor

Employers pay half of Social Security and Medicare for W-2 employees. Independent contractors pay both halves as self-employment tax, 15.3% on most net earnings, and usually make quarterly estimated tax payments. As a rough rule, a contract rate needs to be well above an employee's hourly rate just to break even, before counting unpaid time between projects.

A simple comparison checklist

  1. Convert each offer to annual gross pay, using realistic hours and paid weeks.
  2. Add the value of overtime, match and PTO where they apply.
  3. Subtract costs you would pay yourself, such as insurance, self-employment tax and equipment.
  4. Compare take-home pay with the take-home pay calculator.
