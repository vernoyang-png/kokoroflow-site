What problem does this mortgage calculator solve?

It estimates a monthly principal-and-interest payment from a home price, down payment, interest rate, and loan term, and shows how an extra monthly payment toward principal could shorten the payoff timeline and reduce total interest. It gives a quick planning figure before you talk to a lender.

How to estimate your payment

  1. Enter the home price you're considering.
  2. Enter your planned down payment.
  3. Enter the annual interest rate (APR) and loan term in years.
  4. Optionally enter an extra monthly principal payment.
  5. Calculate to see the scheduled payment, adjusted payment, and estimated total interest.

Privacy, inputs, and limits

  • Upload: No loan documents or personal financial details are uploaded; the estimate runs in your browser.
  • Input: Home price, down payment, rate, term, and optional extra payment.
  • Output: Principal-and-interest estimate only.
  • Property tax, homeowners insurance, HOA fees, PMI, and closing costs are not included.
  • Your actual rate and terms depend on your lender, credit profile, and loan type.

Example

A $400,000 home with an $80,000 down payment (a $320,000 loan) at 6.75% APR over 30 years comes to a scheduled principal-and-interest payment of about $2,075.51 a month, with roughly $427,000 in total interest paid over the full term. Adding even $200 extra toward principal each month can meaningfully shorten the payoff timeline and reduce total interest paid — the calculator's extra-payment field shows this effect directly.

Frequently asked questions

Does this include property tax and insurance?

No. This calculator estimates principal and interest only. Property tax, homeowners insurance, HOA fees, and mortgage insurance (PMI) are separate costs that typically get added to a full monthly housing payment.

How much does an extra payment actually help?

Extra principal payments reduce the loan balance faster, which reduces the interest that accrues on it going forward. Even a modest consistent extra payment can shave years off a 30-year term and save a meaningful amount in total interest.

Is 20% down payment required?

Not necessarily — many loan programs allow smaller down payments, though a down payment below 20% often triggers private mortgage insurance (PMI), which is not included in this estimate.

Can I use this to compare loan terms?

Yes — try 15 years versus 30 years at the same rate to compare the monthly payment against total interest paid, which is a common trade-off when choosing a mortgage term.
