Privacy

Processing happens in your browser. Your images are not sent to KokoroFlow or stored on a server.

Limits

  • Very large images may exceed your device memory.
  • PNG ignores the quality slider; choose WebP or JPG for stronger compression.
  • Animated images are exported as a single frame.

What problem does this image compressor solve?

Large photos and graphics can slow down websites, exceed email attachment limits, and take longer to share. This tool creates a smaller copy in JPG, PNG, or WebP while letting you control output dimensions and quality.

How to compress an image

  1. Choose one or more JPG, PNG, or WebP images.
  2. Select an output format and quality level.
  3. Set maximum width and height if you also want to resize.
  4. Select Compress images.
  5. Review the result and download the new copy.

Privacy, formats, and limits

  • Upload: Images are processed locally in your browser and are not uploaded to KokoroFlow.
  • Input: JPG/JPEG, PNG, and WebP supported by your browser.
  • Output: JPG, PNG, or WebP.
  • Size: No fixed server limit. Available device memory is the practical limit; large batches may fail on mobile devices.
  • PNG is lossless and does not use the quality slider. Animated images become one still frame.

Example

A 4,000 × 3,000 JPG intended for a blog can be limited to 2,000 × 1,500 pixels and saved as WebP at 80% quality. The exact file-size reduction depends on the original image.

Frequently asked questions

Will compression reduce image quality?

Lossy JPG and WebP compression can soften fine detail, especially at low quality settings. Keep the original and inspect the downloaded result.

Can this make a blurry image sharper?

No. Compression and resizing cannot restore detail that is missing from the source image.

Why is the PNG not much smaller?

PNG uses lossless compression. Photographs are often smaller as JPG or WebP, while PNG remains useful for graphics and transparency.
