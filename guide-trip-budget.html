Travel planning guide

Build a trip budget that includes the easy-to-miss costs

Separate fixed costs, daily costs, optional spending, and a buffer so one change does not hide the whole plan.

Reviewed: October 2026 · 6 minute read

Start with fixed costs

List transport to the destination, accommodation bookings, visas, insurance, event tickets, and any reservation already priced. Record whether a quoted room rate includes local taxes, resort charges, cleaning fees, or breakfast.

Build a daily allowance

Estimate food, local transport, and small purchases per person per day. A daily allowance makes it easier to shorten or extend the trip without rebuilding everything. Separate shared costs, such as a hotel room, from per-person costs.

Keep optional spending visible

Activities and shopping are easier to control when they have their own limit. Mark each item as essential, preferred, or optional. If prices rise, remove optional items before reducing essentials such as insurance or safe transport.

Commonly missed costs

  • Checked luggage and seat selection.
  • Airport transfers, parking, tolls, and public-transport cards.
  • Local accommodation taxes and service charges.
  • Card, ATM, roaming, and currency-conversion fees.
  • Tips, laundry, medication, and replacement essentials.
  • Pet care or home costs while away.

Add a contingency buffer

A buffer of your choosing can absorb moderate price changes or an unplanned meal or journey. It does not replace appropriate insurance or emergency savings. Keep emergency funds separate from the normal holiday spending limit.

Review in two currencies

If traveling abroad, keep the booking currency and home currency visible. Exchange rates move, and card providers may use different rates and fees. Recheck major costs shortly before payment.

In depth

Going further: a worked budget and the fees that add up

A trip budget works best as a few separate buckets you can adjust independently. Here is a worked example for two people, followed by the payment fees that most often push a trip over budget.

Worked example: two people, five nights

BucketCalculationAmount
Flights2 × $380$760
Lodging5 nights × $160, plus 14% taxes and fees$912
Food and local transport2 people × 6 days × $70$840
ActivitiesFixed cap$300
Buffer10% of the subtotal$281
Total$3,093 (about $1,547 per person)

All prices are placeholders. Replace them with real quotes, and remember to count travel days: a five-night trip usually involves six days of meals.

Payment fees to check before you go

  • Foreign transaction fees. Some credit and debit cards add a percentage to purchases made in other currencies, and others charge none. Check your card's terms.
  • Dynamic currency conversion. A card terminal abroad may offer to charge you in U.S. dollars. Choosing the local currency usually avoids a less favorable exchange rate set by the merchant's provider.
  • ATM fees. The local ATM operator and your own bank may both charge a fee, so fewer, larger withdrawals usually cost less.
  • Resort and cleaning fees. These are often added after the nightly rate. Compare the total stay price, not the headline rate.

Tracking during the trip

Write down the daily allowance and check it each evening. If one day runs over, take it from the activities bucket or the buffer, not from tomorrow's food money.
